\begin{abstract}
Large language models have made it genuinely possible to build useful question-answering
and clinical decision-support tools --- something that seemed out of reach just a few
years ago.
Getting these systems to actually be trustworthy, however, is considerably harder than
it first appears.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is the most widely adopted approach to that
problem: instead of relying on knowledge baked into model weights at training time,
it fetches relevant documents at query time and conditions the response on what
was actually retrieved.

This thesis looks at how different RAG architectures compare when put to work on a
concrete medical problem: detecting potential drug--drug interactions (DDI) for
patients prescribed multiple medications by different specialists.
Eight families of RAG architectures are surveyed, ranging from the basic
retrieve-then-generate pipeline all the way to Self-RAG, Corrective RAG, and
multi-agent debate systems.
The thesis also introduces RARE-RAG (\textit{Risk-Aware Routed Evidence-graph RAG}),
a novel architecture that classifies query complexity before deciding which pipeline
to run, retrieves evidence using hybrid BM25-plus-vector search, picks the most
complementary set of passages rather than just the top-ranked ones, and verifies
grounding before returning a response --- or explicitly declines to answer when the
evidence is too thin.

Experiments on two benchmarks --- HotpotQA ($9 \times 1{,}000$ question--answer
pairs) and a purpose-built DDI benchmark ($9 \times 100$ queries across five
clinical difficulty levels --- show that RARE-RAG achieves the highest faithfulness
(0.976) on HotpotQA and the best answer correctness on the hardest DDI questions
(L5, expert PK/PD: 0.470).
For the medical domain specifically, Query Rewriting turns out to offer the best
overall quality--latency balance (faithfulness 0.795, answer correctness 0.539).
All nine architectures were evaluated on the same open-source microservice platform,
comprising 14 independently deployable services with configurable RAG strategies
and RAGAS-based automated evaluation.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Large Language Models,
drug--drug interaction detection, clinical decision support, RARE-RAG,
query complexity routing, faithfulness, RAGAS evaluation, microservices,
HotpotQA, DrugBank, polypharmacy
\end{abstract}
