% ---- Abstract ----------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
One of the most powerful applications enabled by large language models (LLMs) is
sophisticated question-answering, advisory, and decision support.
The quality of such applications can be significantly enhanced through
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) --- a technique that grounds model responses
in external, domain-specific knowledge retrieved at inference time.

This thesis presents a technology analysis of state-of-the-art RAG architectures
and the design of an experimental advisory system for the medical domain,
specifically targeting the discovery of potential drug--drug interactions (DDI).
We survey eight families of RAG architectures --- from the vanilla
retrieve-then-generate pipeline through Self-RAG, Corrective RAG, and agentic
multi-agent approaches --- and introduce RARE-RAG
(\textit{Risk-Aware Routed Evidence-graph RAG}), a novel architecture that
combines query complexity routing, hybrid evidence retrieval, set-wise evidence
selection, and a grounding verifier with abstention capability.

An empirical evaluation on the HotpotQA benchmark (9{,}000 question--answer pairs
across nine RAG modes) shows that RARE-RAG achieves the highest faithfulness score
(0.976) among all evaluated architectures, while maintaining competitive answer
accuracy (Token~F1: 0.543). The system is implemented as an open-source
microservice platform comprising 14 services with configurable RAG strategies.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Large Language Models,
drug interaction detection, decision support systems, multi-agent RAG, faithfulness,
RAGAS evaluation, microservices, HotpotQA
\end{abstract}
