\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Consider a patient in their sixties managing three chronic conditions: atrial fibrillation,
type~2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease.
Three separate specialists --- a cardiologist, an endocrinologist, and a nephrologist ---
each prescribe their own regimen.
The result is a daily combination of seven or more drugs, often without any single
physician having reviewed the complete medication list for cross-drug interactions.
Polypharmacy of this kind affects an estimated 40\% of patients over 65 in developed
countries~\cite{masnoon2017polypharmacy}, and drug--drug interactions (DDIs) contribute
to tens of thousands of preventable hospital admissions each year~\cite{lazarou1998ade}.

Modern large language models (LLMs) are fluent, broad-knowledge reasoners that can, in
principle, answer clinical questions about drug safety.
Yet they are not safe for this task out of the box: they hallucinate, their training
knowledge has a cutoff date, and they cannot explain \emph{why} a specific interaction
exists based on citable evidence.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses these weaknesses by grounding every
response in retrieved source documents, making answers traceable and updatable without
retraining the model.

This thesis investigates how different RAG architectures perform on a multi-hop
question-answering benchmark, proposes a novel architecture tailored for high-stakes
advisory tasks, and demonstrates the approach through a prototype drug interaction
advisory system.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Three research questions guide this work:

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1] Which RAG architecture achieves the best trade-off between answer quality
    (Token~F1, Exact Match) and latency on a multi-hop reasoning benchmark?

  \item[RQ2] Does a routing-aware, set-wise evidence selection approach with a grounding
    verifier (\rarerag{}) achieve higher faithfulness than single-path RAG architectures?

  \item[RQ3] Can a configurable microservice RAG platform serve as a reliable technology
    demonstrator for drug interaction advisory, with results that are traceable to source
    documents?
\end{description}

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

This thesis covers a survey of eight RAG architecture families
(\cref{cha:background}), the design of \rarerag{} as a novel four-component
architecture (\cref{cha:rare_rag}), implementation of a 14-service microservice
platform with configurable RAG strategies
(\crefrange{cha:system_design}{cha:implementation}), and an empirical benchmark
on HotpotQA across nine RAG modes with 1{,}000 questions each
(\cref{cha:evaluation}).

The following are explicitly out of scope: clinical validation or patient-facing
deployment, regulatory compliance (MDR, FDA SaMD guidance), fine-tuning or pre-training
of language models, and evaluation on the drug interaction corpus --- the latter is
planned as future work, with infrastructure and data preparation scripts already available
in the repository.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The main contributions of this thesis are:

The first contribution is a \textbf{comparative empirical study} of nine RAG pipeline
variants on HotpotQA (9{,}000 evaluated questions), using both lexical metrics
(Token~F1, EM, ROUGE-L) and RAGAS-based indicators (faithfulness, answer relevance,
context recall), together with latency and cost analysis.
The second is the \textbf{\rarerag{} architecture} --- a novel RAG pipeline combining
query complexity routing, hybrid BM25+vector retrieval, set-wise evidence selection,
and a grounding verifier with abstention; \rarerag{} achieves the highest faithfulness
score (0.976) across all evaluated modes.
The third is the \textbf{open-source \medrag{} platform} --- a production-grade
microservice system comprising 14 services with configurable chunking strategies,
embedding providers, and RAG modes, backed by a full CI/CD pipeline and Docker
deployment.

\section{Thesis Organisation}
\label{sec:organisation}

The remainder of this thesis is organised as follows.
\cref{cha:background} surveys the evolution of RAG from the original
retrieve-then-generate paradigm through agentic multi-agent systems and positions this
work within the literature.
\cref{cha:rare_rag} presents the \rarerag{} architecture in detail, covering its
four components and the hypotheses it addresses.
\cref{cha:system_design} documents the full microservice architecture of the
\medrag{} platform using C4 diagrams and sequence models.
\cref{cha:implementation} describes selected non-trivial implementation decisions:
embedding abstraction, hybrid search tuning, SSE streaming, and evaluation integration.
\cref{cha:evaluation} presents the experimental setup, results, and discussion.
\cref{cha:conclusions} summarises the findings, revisits the research questions,
and outlines directions for future work.
